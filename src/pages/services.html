What we publish, and what is in it

Four desks, one note format, one disclosure standard. This page sets out exactly what each desk covers so you can judge the fit before you pay for anything.

The desks

Where we do the work

Each desk below publishes to the same note format and the same disclosure rules. The lists are what the research actually examines — not a feature comparison.

Fundamental equity research

Bottom-up company work on listed Indian equities. We read the annual report and the last eight quarters before forming a view, and the note shows the arithmetic rather than asserting the conclusion.

  • Business model and revenue quality
  • Margin structure, cash conversion and working capital
  • Leverage, contingent liabilities and related-party exposure
  • Valuation across a range, not a single target

Technical research

Structure-based work on price and volume. Used to time entries into fundamentally screened names, and to set the level at which a thesis is mechanically abandoned.

  • Trend, structure and volume confirmation
  • Relative strength against index and sector
  • Defined invalidation level on every setup
  • Timeframe stated explicitly — positional or short term

Derivatives research

Index and single-stock futures and options. Because the loss profile here can exceed the premium paid, every note carries its margin and payoff assumptions in full.

  • Strategy structure and net payoff at expiry
  • Margin requirement and worst-case loss stated up front
  • Open interest and implied volatility context
  • Adjustment and exit conditions defined before entry

Macro & market commentary

Context rather than calls. Published on a fixed cadence so you can read the market's condition without waiting for something to trade.

  • Domestic data, policy and rate expectations
  • Sector rotation and breadth
  • Institutional flow and positioning
  • Results-season scorecards against expectation

Note anatomy

Nine things in every published note

The format is fixed so that nothing important can be quietly omitted — including the parts that are inconvenient for us.

Mandatory sections of a Wealthymind research note.
Section What it contains
Instrument Security name, ISIN, exchange and segment.
Thesis The case, in plain language, in no more than a paragraph.
Evidence Sourced figures and references to the filings relied on.
Counter-case The strongest argument against the thesis.
Risk & invalidation The level or event at which the idea is abandoned.
Timeframe The horizon over which the thesis is expected to play out.
Review date When the note will be revisited, closed or re-rated.
Disclosures Analyst and firm holdings, and any other conflict of interest.
Disclaimer Standard regulatory disclaimer and risk warning.

What a note is not

It is not personalised advice. Our notes are distributed identically to every subscriber on a plan and take no account of your financial position, tax situation or goals. Position sizing is your decision, and for advice specific to your circumstances you should consult a SEBI registered investment adviser.

Delivery

How research reaches you

Email, as the record

Every note is delivered by email to the address on your subscription. Email is the authoritative copy and is timestamped.

Fixed publication windows

Notes are released in defined windows rather than continuously, so you are not pushed into reacting intraday to an alert.

Closure notices

When an idea is closed or re-rated you receive a separate note saying so, with the reason. Open positions are never orphaned.
